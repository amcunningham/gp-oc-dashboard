## Key observations

- **Three sub-10% response outliers, all sent the largest mailings.** GP Pathfinder Clinics (3.7%), Brownlow Group Practice (6.8%) and GP at Hand (7.9%) returned the survey at roughly a third or less of the national 22.1% rate — yet were sent the three largest samples of the whole group (1,090 / 1,120 / 1,094). All three are digital-first or highly transient-population single-code practices with very large registered lists.
- **Everyone else returns normally (16–37%).** Every conventional at-scale partnership and super-partnership division sits at or above the low-20s, i.e. around or above the national rate, off smaller mailings (~460–850).
- **Ipsos increases the sample where response is poor — and it doesn't recover the base.** Pathfinder: sent ~919 (2024) -> ~945 (2025) -> 1,090 (2026) as its response rate fell 8.6% -> 5.5% -> 3.7%; completed responses fell 79 -> 52 -> 40 despite the growing mailing. GP at Hand shows the same drift (RR 10.4 -> 9.1 -> 7.9; completed 94 -> 89 -> 86). You cannot sample your way out of a list that will not answer.
- **Implication for practice-level analysis.** The estimates for the sub-10% cohort (Pathfinder n=40, Brownlow n=76, GP at Hand n=86 out of registered lists of 59k–99k) are barely powered and carry very wide uncertainty. These practices should be flagged (and probably excluded) wherever practice-level GPPS estimates feed the models, and they drop out of continuity analysis by construction (too few patients report a preferred GP to publish the routed question).
