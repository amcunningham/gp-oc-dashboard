Ask the GP Access Research Data

Natural-language querying of practice-level open data on GP appointment access in England (6,007 practices; monthly panel Mar 2023–May 2026). Your question is turned into SQL by a language model, the SQL is always shown, and the query runs entirely in your browser with DuckDB-WASM — no data leaves this page. See the data dictionary and analysis notes.

What data is behind this? What can I ask?

~6,000 English GP practices, in two tables:

Monthly trends, March 2023 – May 2026 — appointment volumes, same-day vs booked-ahead mix, waiting bands, and online consultation use, for every practice, every month.

A practice profile snapshot — staffing, population, patient experience, quality and prescribing measures. These are anchored to the year ending March 2025, because the patient survey they centre on was fielded in early 2025; the matching appointment measures cover the same year so that like is compared with like.

Built entirely from open data:

  • Appointments in General Practice — volumes, booking-to-appointment waits, mode, staff type
  • Online Consultation Submissions — OC use, suppliers, and time-of-day of submissions
  • Cloud Based Telephony — call volumes and the 8–10am phone rush
  • GP Patient Survey 2025 — satisfaction, continuity, experienced access, respondent demographics
  • General Practice Workforce — GP/nurse/other FTEs, registered population age
  • NHS Payments to General Practice — rurality, dispensing status
  • OHID Fingertips — IMD deprivation, QOF points and diabetes prevalence, cancer detection/referral measures (NDRS)
  • NHSBSA English Prescribing Data — prescription items, antibiotics
  • NHS ODS — practice postcodes, closures

Full build pipeline and derived files: data dictionary · GitHub.

Good questions look like: a place ("access in Brighton", "compare Cardiff Road Surgery with its PCN" — practices are named), a comparison ("deprived vs affluent", "rural vs urban", "big vs small"), a trend ("how have long waits changed since 2023?"), a relationship ("does phone consulting relate to antibiotic prescribing?"), or a findings question ("what predicts continuity?") which is answered from the research notes.

It can't see: individual patients or GPs (everything is practice-level), hospital data beyond the cancer measures, anything outside England, or events after May 2026.

Model settings (needed once — stored only in your browser)

Shared demo: free to use, rate-limited (20 questions/hour), and questions are logged anonymously to improve the tool — don't include personal details. Own key: Anthropic keys work directly from the browser; nothing is logged. Keys are kept in localStorage on your machine only. Local models: run ollama serve with OLLAMA_ORIGINS=*, pick OpenAI-compatible, model e.g. llama3.1, leave key blank.

Read before interpreting: this is practice-level (ecological) open data — associations, not causes. GPAD's "same day" measures how practices configure booking (total-triage practices book everything same-day), not what patients experience; the two are nearly uncorrelated. GPAD is official statistics in development. GPPS is a weighted sample survey. From April 2026 contract changes alter same-day recording. Full caveats in the notes.
