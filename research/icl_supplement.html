Supplement: where the inverse care law operates in English general practice

Full tables underlying the post. All figures are from the GP Patient Survey (GPPS) 2026 analysis tool unless stated, weighted to the registered adult population of England, restricted to the most affected group the survey identifies (a health condition reduces day-to-day activities "a lot") and — for the contact-experience rows — to contacts in the last three months, so patients are recalling a recent event. Percentages are rounded; weighted bases (n) are shown so any rate can be checked. I write in a personal capacity; this is not produced or endorsed by NHS England.

How to read these. The post shows one headline cascade for the most affected patients, most-deprived fifth against least. This page gives the same measures broken down further — by how severely limited the patient is, by the condition they have, by the reason they contacted, and over time — plus the administrative and practice-level analyses. None of the breakdowns changes the conclusion in the post: the deprivation gradient in being deferred or redirected sits on top of everything else.

The need behind this: severe limitation by deprivation

Day-to-day activity limitation is the survey's own measure of need, and it is heavily concentrated. In the most deprived fifth, 25,090 (weighted) respondents report a condition that limits them "a lot" — two and a half times the 10,009 in the least deprived fifth. The mirror image holds for "not at all", which rises from 23,791 to 33,805 as deprivation falls, while "a little" stays broadly flat: among people who have a condition, its severity shifts down the gradient. As a share of everyone with a long-term condition, severe limitation runs from 31% in the most deprived fifth to 14% in the least.

8. The capacity link — practice-level association

Across 5,928 English practices, the share of surveyed contacts told to come back another day is higher where deprivation and recorded morbidity are higher and, independently, where there are fewer GPs and fewer appointments per registered patient. These are cross-sectional associations, not causal estimates; they are consistent with the reading that the deferral gradient reflects capacity meeting demand, and they rule out the competing explanations (age structure, contact route, reason for contact, condition mix) that the survey breakdowns above address.

Confidence limits

Every interval and error bar on this page is a 95% confidence limit for a proportion, calculated as p ± 1.96 × √(p(1−p)/n), where p is the estimated proportion and n is its weighted base. On the charts of counts the same limits are shown scaled to counts (±1.96 × √(k(n−k)/n) for a count k). Because the weighted bases here run into the tens of thousands, the limits are narrow — typically under ±0.4 percentage points, or a few hundred on the count scale. They are binomial and take no account of the survey's weighting design effect; under a conservative design effect of two they would widen by about 40%, which changes no conclusion on this page.
