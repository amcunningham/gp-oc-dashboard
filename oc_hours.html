Do practices that leave online consultation open 24/7 have less of an 8am rush?

When practices move to online consultation, the 8am phone rush is often replaced by an 8am online rush (see the 8am Rush analysis). One explanation offered is that many practices only open their online consultation tool to requests from 8am, so if they accepted requests around the clock the rush would go away. The alternative is capacity: if same-day care is allocated each morning and runs out, patients have a reason to get in early, whenever the front door opens.

Some practices already take OC requests 24/7, which allows the two explanations to be tested. This page groups practices by when their OC submissions actually arrive and compares their calls, OC submissions, appointments and patient experience in September 2026.

Slightly less, but there is still a morning peak. In practices open 24/7 for clinical requests, 29.0% of weekday calls and OC submissions arrive between 8 and 10am, against 30.9% in practices open in core hours only. Most of the difference is in OC. Practices with similar OC use show the same difference. Opening OC at 7am does about as well (28.0%); 24/7 opening for admin requests only makes little difference (29.7%).

The team's morning workload is the same. Requests sent overnight or at the weekend are waiting at 8am, so every group handles about a third of its week's contacts between 8 and 10am.

The results fit the capacity explanation better than the opening-hours one. Keeping OC open around the clock changes the morning peak only slightly; practices with a smaller peak also offer more appointments that can be booked ahead.

Patients contact 24/7 clinical practices as often as other practices, but more of them phone. Adjusted for deprivation, age, list size and region, these practices get 2.4 more calls per 1,000 per weekday (95% CI +0.6 to +4.3), 2.9 fewer OC submissions (−3.7 to −2.1), and no difference in total. Evening and weekend submissions replace contacts that would otherwise come in core hours, rather than adding to them.

Deprivation does not explain this. Contact rates rise with deprivation (32.4 to 37.5 per 1,000 per weekday across fifths in core-hours-only practices), but the groups have a similar deprivation mix (median IMD 19.1 vs 20.6). In every deprivation fifth, 24/7 clinical practices get about a third as much OC (2.3–3.8 vs 7.6–10.2 per 1,000); adjusted for deprivation, age, list size, region and supplier, about half (ratio 0.50, 95% CI 0.43 to 0.58). After adjustment, there is no difference in total contacts (−0.1 per 1,000 per weekday, 95% CI −1.9 to +1.6).

24/7 clinical practices answer slightly more calls, clearest at 8–10am (+3.9 points adjusted). Their patients report higher satisfaction, but after adjustment only ease of getting through by phone is clearly higher (+3.6 points); the other extended-hours groups show similar gains.

What this means

  • Patients still send requests early. Even when OC is open around the clock, 8–9am is the busiest hour. The share of phone calls arriving at 8–10am is similar to that in other practices.
  • The morning peak reflects limited clinical capacity. Clinical requests are concentrated early in the morning and admin requests are not. Admin can be dealt with without an appointment; clinical requests compete for limited same-day appointments. This is why admin-only 24/7 opening makes little difference.
  • The team's morning workload is unchanged, and Monday is heavier where OC is open 24/7.
  • The practices with the smallest morning peak offer more booked-ahead appointments, and their patients find it easier to get through by phone. This reflects how they organise access generally; it cannot be attributed to OC opening hours alone.

Limits

  • This is a cross-sectional comparison. Practices choose their hours, and the 24/7 clinical group is small and mostly low-OC.
  • Opening hours are inferred from timestamps, not read from practice settings. A stricter definition of the 24/7 clinical group gave the same results in an earlier run on June 2026 data.
  • June 2026 OC with May 2026 telephony gave the same pattern as September 2026.
  • The figures are monthly averages. Mondays will be heavier, because they carry the weekend's OC.
  • Some suppliers don't code requests as clinical or admin, which limits the admin-only vs clinical split.
