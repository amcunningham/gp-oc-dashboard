Do practices that leave online consultation open 24/7 have less of an 8am rush?

A follow-up to the 8am Rush analysis. Opening hours are inferred from when submissions actually arrive, then compared with each practice's call pattern.

A little, but they still have one. Practices open 24/7 for clinical requests have a smaller share of their weekday calls and OC submissions arriving between 8 and 10am: 30.3% against 32.4% in practices open in core hours only. The gap holds within each OC-volume band. Most of the difference is in OC; the phone rush barely changes. Opening OC at 7am on weekdays does slightly better (29.0%). Being open 24/7 for admin requests only makes little difference (30.9%).

For the practice team, the 8am peak doesn't go away. Requests sent overnight or at the weekend are waiting in the queue when triage starts at 8am. What changes is when patients send them. If everything queued overnight is handled between 8 and 10am, every group faces about a third of its week's work in those two hours (33.2% for 24/7 clinical, 32.4% for core hours only).

When OC submissions arrive

OC submissions per 1,000 patients per weekday, in the same two-hour bands as the call and combined charts, so the three can be read together: calls plus OC equals all contacts. The 24/7 clinical group gets far fewer OC submissions at 8–10am (1.4 vs 3.2 per 1,000). Mostly that is because they get less OC overall. Their timing differs too. Only 13% of their OC arrives in the 8–9am hour, against 26% in core-hours-only practices, and 8% arrives on weekday evenings. At weekends they get 1.1 per 1,000 per day, against none in core-hours-only practices.

When patients phone

Opening all day does not bring in more contacts overall. Across the full 24 hours, the 24/7 clinical group gets 33.1 contacts per 1,000 per weekday, against 34.5 in core-hours-only practices. Over a week, including weekend OC, the totals are 167 and 172 per 1,000. Evening and weekend submissions seem to replace contacts that would otherwise come in core hours, rather than add to them. One exception: the 27 high-OC practices open 24/7 for clinical requests have a median of 40.6 contacts per 1,000 per weekday, against 34.3 for high-OC core-hours-only practices. That group is too small to draw conclusions from.

What the team faces at 8am. The last two rows assume OC that arrives out of hours (weekday evenings, overnight, before 8am and at weekends) is dealt with between 8 and 10am the next working morning, along with calls and OC arriving in that window. The denominator is everything the practice has to respond to in a week: calls in core hours plus all OC. Calls outside core hours are left out because nobody answers them. On this basis, every group faces about a third of its week's work between 8 and 10am. The 24/7 clinical group is at 33.2%, against 32.4% for core-hours-only practices. Out-of-hours opening spreads out when patients send requests, but the requests pile up and land in the same morning window. For OC alone, nearly half of all requests in practices with longer opening hours are waiting at 8–10am, against 40% where OC opens at 8am.

Reading it

  • The patient's race is smaller, not gone. Clinical requests can now arrive in the evening and at weekends. Weekday 18:30–midnight takes 8% of submissions and weekends 7%. Patients still send more requests between 8 and 9am than in any other hour, which fits the idea that early requests are a rational response when same-day care is allocated each morning.
  • The phone rush barely moves. Out-of-hours OC changes when OC users send requests. It does little for the people who phone.
  • Opening at 7am does about as well (29.0% of contacts at 8–10am), by moving part of the race an hour earlier.
  • Admin-only 24/7 opening has little effect (30.9%). This fits the earlier finding that clinical requests race and admin requests don't.
  • The Monday peak is only slightly smaller. The ratio of Monday to Tuesday–Friday contacts at 8–10am falls from 1.56 by about 0.04 in both the 24/7 clinical and early-opening groups.

Limits

  • This is a cross-sectional comparison. Practices choose their hours, and the 24/7 clinical group is small and mostly low-OC.
  • Opening hours are inferred from timestamps, not read from practice settings. A stricter definition (clinical requests on all 8 weekend days and overnight on ≥10 nights, n = 97) gives the same results.
  • The OC data is from June 2026 and the telephony data from May 2026. Later editions are published but could not be downloaded for this run.
  • Some suppliers don't code requests as clinical or admin, which limits the admin-only vs clinical split.
