Do practices that leave online consultation open 24/7 have less of an 8am rush?

When practices move to online consultation, the 8am phone rush is often replaced by an 8am online rush (see the 8am Rush analysis). One explanation offered is that many practices only open their online consultation tool to requests from 8am, so if they accepted requests around the clock the rush would go away. The alternative is capacity: if same-day care is allocated each morning and runs out, patients have a reason to get in early, whenever the front door opens.

Some practices already take OC requests 24/7, which allows the two explanations to be tested. This page groups practices by when their OC submissions actually arrive and compares their calls, OC submissions, appointments and patient experience in September 2026.

Not really. When practices opened their OC to requests overnight and at weekends, the share of their calls arriving at 8–10am did not fall (+0.4 points, 95% CI −0.4 to +1.3; 24 practices). When practices closed their OC out of hours, the share of calls and OC arriving at 8–10am rose by less than one point (+0.7; 170 practices).

Comparing practices gives the same picture. In practices open 24/7 for clinical requests, 28.9% of weekday calls and OC submissions arrive between 8 and 10am, against 30.9% in practices open in core hours only. The difference is in OC; their phone peak is no smaller. Opening 24/7 for admin requests only makes little difference (29.7%).

The team's morning workload is the same. Requests sent overnight or at the weekend are dealt with when the practice opens, so every group handles about a third of its week's contacts between 8 and 10am.

The results fit the capacity explanation better than the opening-hours one. Practices with a smaller morning peak also offer more appointments that can be booked ahead.

Practices that changed their hours

The clearest test is what happened when practices changed their OC hours. Opening OC overnight and at weekends did not reduce the share of calls arriving at 8–10am, and closing it did not increase it. Each practice's change is measured against practices that kept the same hours over the same months.

Opening OC out of hours moves some OC requests out of the 8–10am window, and closing it moves them back, but the phone peak does not change and the combined effect is under one percentage point. Few practices opened OC out of hours, so a small effect cannot be ruled out.

Since January 2025 more practices have stopped taking OC requests out of hours than have started. Most practices now open 24/7 for clinical requests have been that way for some time: of the 172 in September 2026, 56 were already open 24/7 in January 2025.

When contacts arrive

Per 1,000 patients per weekday, in two-hour bands. The three charts add up: calls plus OC equals all contacts.

OC submissions

24/7 clinical practices get less OC at 8–10am (1.4 vs 3.2 per 1,000), mainly because they get less OC overall. 14% of their OC arrives 8–9am, against 26% in core-hours-only practices; 8% arrives on weekday evenings and they get 1.1 per 1,000 per weekend day.

Even where requests can be sent at any time, few are sent overnight or at weekends. A request sent in the evening is usually not dealt with until the next working day, and practices generally advise patients to use 111 for urgent problems out of hours. If same-day care is allocated from 8am, there is little to gain by sending a request earlier, which may be why 8–9am is still the busiest hour.

Phone calls

24/7 clinical practices get the most 8–10am calls (7.4 vs 6.9 per 1,000), because most rely mainly on the phone.

Booked-ahead appointments and the morning peak

The same pattern holds across all practices, not only the four groups. Across 4,746 practices with September 2026 phone, OC and appointments data, the morning peak is smaller where more appointments are booked ahead. It is only weakly related to the total number of appointments, and not related to the number of GPs.

The differences are modest, and the direction could run both ways: a practice with a heavy morning peak may release more appointments on the day.

Check: broader groups

The four groups leave out many practices whose pattern is less clear-cut. The conclusions are the same. Practices open 24/7 receive a slightly smaller share of calls and OC at 8–10am, but their phone peak is no smaller, and their team handles as large a share of the week's contacts at the start of the day, with a heavier Monday. With all 24/7 practices together, the lower same-day booking seen in the 24/7 clinical group disappears: that difference belongs to the phone-first practices in that group rather than to 24/7 opening.

What this means

  • Patients still send requests early. Even when OC is open around the clock, 8–9am is the busiest hour. The share of phone calls arriving at 8–10am is similar to that in other practices.
  • The morning peak reflects limited clinical capacity. Clinical requests are concentrated early in the morning and admin requests are not. Admin can be dealt with without an appointment; clinical requests compete for limited same-day appointments. Limited capacity also explains why admin-only 24/7 opening makes little difference.
  • The team's morning workload is unchanged, and Monday is heavier where OC is open 24/7.
  • The practices with the smallest morning peak offer more booked-ahead appointments, and their patients find it easier to get through by phone. These differences reflect how the practices organise access generally and cannot be attributed to OC opening hours alone.

Limits

  • Practices choose their hours. Most comparisons are between different practices at one point in time, and the 24/7 clinical group is small (145 practices in September 2026) and mostly low-OC. Practices that changed their hours give a before-and-after check, but there are few of them and they may have changed other things at the same time.
  • Opening hours are inferred. Groups are based on when submissions arrive, not on practice settings.
  • Some suppliers record times in UTC. During British Summer Time, Anima (Continuum Health), Blinx and Rapid Health, and eConsult in September 2026, record OC submission times an hour earlier than UK clock time. Their times have been moved forward an hour. The phone systems of about 95 practices, mostly in Birmingham and Solihull, the Black Country and Leeds, do the same for calls; these practices are left out of the time-of-day call measures. The summer 2024 files show the same problem for most suppliers and cannot be corrected, so the history starts in January 2025.
  • Clinical and admin coding is incomplete. Some suppliers do not code requests as clinical or admin, which limits the split between admin-only and clinical 24/7 opening.
