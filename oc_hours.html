Do practices that leave online consultation open 24/7 have less of an 8am rush?

A follow-up to the 8am Rush analysis. Opening hours are inferred from when OC submissions actually arrive, then compared with each practice's calls in the same month.

A little, but they still have one. Practices open 24/7 for clinical requests have a smaller share of their weekday calls and OC submissions arriving between 8 and 10am: 29.0%, against 30.9% in practices open in core hours only. The gap holds within each OC-volume band. Most of the difference is in OC, not phone calls. Opening OC at 7am on weekdays does slightly better (28.0%). Being open 24/7 for admin requests only makes little difference (29.7%).

For the practice team, the 8am peak doesn't go away. Requests sent overnight or at the weekend are waiting in the queue when triage starts at 8am. What changes is when patients send them. If everything queued overnight is handled between 8 and 10am, every group faces about a third of its week's contacts in those two hours (31.9% for 24/7 clinical, 30.9% for core hours only).

When OC submissions arrive

OC submissions per 1,000 patients per weekday, in the same two-hour bands as the call and combined charts, so the three can be read together: calls plus OC equals all contacts. The 24/7 clinical group gets far fewer OC submissions at 8–10am (1.5 vs 3.5 per 1,000). Mostly that is because they get less OC overall. Their timing differs too. Only 14% of their OC arrives in the 8–9am hour, against 26% in core-hours-only practices, and 8% arrives on weekday evenings. At weekends they get 1.1 per 1,000 per day, against none in core-hours-only practices.

Opening all day brings in about the same number of contacts. Across the full 24 hours, the median practice in both the 24/7 clinical and core-hours-only groups gets 34.1 contacts per 1,000 per weekday. Over a week it is 174 and 172 per 1,000. Evening and weekend submissions seem to replace contacts that would otherwise come in core hours, rather than add to them.

Deprivation does not explain it. Contact rates rise steeply with deprivation: core-hours-only practices go from 32.4 contacts per 1,000 per weekday in the least deprived fifth to 37.5 in the most deprived. The 24/7 clinical group has a similar deprivation mix (median IMD score 19.1 vs 20.6), and within each deprivation fifth its rates are not consistently higher or lower. Adjusting step by step, the difference for 24/7 clinical practices is +0.7 per 1,000 per weekday unadjusted, +0.8 after deprivation, −0.1 after adding age, and −0.1 (95% CI −1.9 to +1.6) after also adjusting for list size, region and OC volume. None of these is distinguishable from zero.

One possible exception: the 19 high-OC practices open 24/7 for clinical requests have a median of 38.4 contacts per 1,000 per weekday, against 34.8 for high-OC core-hours-only practices. That group is too small to draw conclusions from.

What the team handles at 8am. The last two rows assume OC that arrives out of hours (weekday evenings, overnight, before 8am and at weekends) is dealt with between 8 and 10am the next working morning, along with calls and OC arriving in that window. The denominator is everything the practice has to respond to in a week: calls in core hours plus all OC. Calls outside core hours are left out because nobody answers them. On this basis, every group handles about a third of its week's contacts between 8 and 10am. The 24/7 clinical group is at 31.9%, against 30.9% for core-hours-only practices. Out-of-hours opening spreads out when patients send requests, but the requests pile up and land in the same morning window. For OC alone, nearly half of all requests in practices with longer opening hours are waiting at 8–10am, against 40% where OC opens at 8am.

Reading it

  • The patient's race is smaller, not gone. Clinical requests can now arrive in the evening and at weekends. Weekday 18:30–midnight takes 8% of submissions and weekends 6%. Patients still send more requests between 8 and 9am than in any other hour, which fits the idea that early requests are a rational response when same-day care is allocated each morning.
  • The phone rush barely moves. Out-of-hours OC changes when OC users send requests. It does little for the people who phone.
  • Opening at 7am does about as well (28.0% of contacts arriving 8–10am), by moving part of the race an hour earlier.
  • Admin-only 24/7 opening has little effect (29.7%). This fits the earlier finding that clinical requests race and admin requests don't.
  • The team's morning is no lighter. Counting the requests that queue overnight, every group handles about a third of its week's contacts between 8 and 10am.

Limits

  • This is a cross-sectional comparison. Practices choose their hours, and the 24/7 clinical group is small and mostly low-OC.
  • Opening hours are inferred from timestamps, not read from practice settings. A stricter definition of the 24/7 clinical group gave the same results in an earlier run on June 2026 data.
  • June 2026 OC with May 2026 telephony gave the same pattern as September 2026.
  • The figures are monthly averages. Mondays will be heavier, because they carry the weekend's OC.
  • Some suppliers don't code requests as clinical or admin, which limits the admin-only vs clinical split.
