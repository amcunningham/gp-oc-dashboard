Does online consultation end the 8am rush?

NHS England · Cloud Based Telephony & OC submissions · February 2026 · per working day

Design: Cross-sectional comparison of 4,306 practices with both telephony (CBT) and OC data, grouped into tertiles by current OC rate (Feb 2026, per 1,000 patients per working day).
Low OC (n=1,435) — <2.1/1k/wd · median 8 submissions/day · essentially phone-first.
Mid OC (n=1,436) — 2.1–9.2/1k/wd · median 43/day · OC as secondary channel.
High OC (n=1,435) — ≥9.3/1k/wd · median 144/day · heavily OC-dependent.
Data: CBT001 = inbound calls. CBT003 = answered. All rates per 1,000 patients per working day (Feb 2026: 20 working days).

Reading: At 8–10am, high-OC practices answer a higher share of calls (58% vs 52%) because the queue is shorter. But from 10am onwards, low-OC practices answer 7–9 percentage points more (64–70% vs 57–63%). The rush-hour advantage does not carry through the day.

Key finding: Total demand per working day is virtually identical across all tertiles (~31–34/1k/wd). High-OC practices receive ~10 fewer phone calls per 1k/wd than low-OC, but ~11 more OC submissions — an almost exact substitution. The number of calls answered is also proportionally stable. OC has not created spare capacity.
Does OC end the 8am rush? No. It moves the rush from the phone queue to the digital inbox. Total 8–10am demand is comparable across tertiles (~10–11/1k/wd). The phone queue is shorter at 8am, which briefly improves the answer rate, but by 10am low-OC practices are answering a higher share of calls.

Any sign of freed-up capacity? Not in these data. Total demand per working day is the same (~31–34/1k) regardless of OC level — phone calls and OC submissions substitute almost one-for-one. If OC were freeing up capacity, we would expect fewer total contacts to process, or more calls answered from a shorter queue, or a higher answer rate outside the rush. None of these are observed. The underlying constraint is clinical capacity: the same patients need triaging and in many cases seeing, regardless of which channel they used.
